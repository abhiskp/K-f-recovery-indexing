# Methods and limits at the Phase 4 evidence gate

## Study design

The first feasible experiment is a **source-level spatial feasibility audit**, not a flood classifier or recovery model. A central-Kerala pilot rectangle, 76.35–76.60°E and 9.45–9.75°N, was fixed to cover the Vembanad/Alappuzha region where both July and August inundation were reported. This rectangle is a screening area, not a crop polygon or administrative unit.

We reconstructed the official event chronology before selecting any satellite baseline. NRSC's 16 July Sentinel-1A map confirms July flooding; its 21 August report lists 16 July Sentinel-1, 21 July Radarsat-2, 9 August Sentinel-1, 11/14 August Radarsat-2, 17 August ALOS-2, 18 August TerraSAR-X/Radarsat-2, and 21 August Radarsat-2. The date of a viewer layer, product issue and image acquisition are distinct. The local chronology CSV records this distinction where details are absent. A July scene can be pre-August yet already flood-exposed.

The Earth Engine query covers June 2016 to 14 October 2018 UTC inclusive (exclusive filter end 15 October), rather than the older July/August windows. It inventories every intersecting Sentinel-1 GRD IW/VV **granule** in the pilot, with acquisition time, platform, pass, orbit, nominal resolution, footprint fraction, VV valid fraction and incidence-angle summary. The 134-granule count is a query result, not 134 independent comparisons. In 2018 the 51 granules represent 27 acquisitions: 24 split into adjacent granules and three single-granule acquisitions. The earlier unexecuted candidate script counted granules rather than acquisitions and could have given false multi-scene confidence. The corrected script mosaics adjacent granules by date before computing date-level medians, so the early and late reference pairs each comprise **two actual acquisitions**. Even matching geometry does not ensure an unflooded pre-event state or eliminate rain-driven soil moisture changes.

The fixed exploratory design uses S1A descending relative orbit 165 on 10/22 June, 4/16 July, and 9/21 August. The first three dates are only **pre-first-documented-flood reference candidates**, not verified dry observations; 16 July is exported for chronology but excluded from both reference pairs. The 9 and 21 August scenes are **different event dates**, not a single temporally pooled inundation map. The script exports their VV decrease against early (10/22 June) and late (22 June/4 July) references, low event backscatter, 2/3/4 dB sensitivity masks, pre-2018 JRC annual water history (2014–17), slope, incidence angle, and explicit validity/overlap layers. The all-years JRC occurrence product would include post-2018 information and is avoided as a baseline. These masks remain unvalidated and no threshold is called calibrated. Urban double-bounce, crop growth, roughness, wind on water, terrain shadow and mixed land cover can alter SAR backscatter independently of flood water. The `angle` band is an approximate incidence-angle diagnostic; its resolution and terrain effects must be considered.

The downloaded 45-band GeoTIFF was audited at 30 m in EPSG:32643. A pilot AOI mask was rasterized from the fixed lon/lat rectangle on the exported grid; counts refer to pixel centers and approximate 900 m² pixel areas, not surveyed land area. The six-row acquisition QA export agrees with the earlier Code Editor transcription and exact inventory scene/orbit IDs. The projected raster contains 1,011,610 pilot pixel centers. Although S1 pre/event overlap covers 99.75% of these, the 2014–17 JRC history is finite at only 36.53%; the strict water/slope/overlap support covers just 23.57%. This spatial missingness makes candidate totals strongly dependent on the support definition. A no-water-exclusion variant covers 78.33% but cannot remove permanent water. The GeoTIFF does not declare a nodata value; analytic bands contain NaNs while candidate-mask bands encode unsupported pixels as zero. The technical export-audit script explicitly uses finite tests and QA bands, so zeros outside support never enter denominators as negatives.

## Exposure-to-rice join

The Phase 3 [district-season rice panel](../phase3/data/processed/district_season_rice_2014_2020.csv) contains area, production and derived yield for 14 districts × 3 harvest seasons × 6 agricultural years. It can provide district-season outcomes and a 2014–15 to 2017–18 same-season descriptive reference. A credible exposure join additionally needs a verified 2018 paddy map (crop/season-specific where possible), 2018-valid district or block boundaries, aligned validated flood extent with per-pixel coverage and uncertainty, and a denominator of eligible rice area. The public OSM district geometry is only a candidate. Without these, no district-level hectares of rice inundated, exposed-versus-less-exposed panel or exposure gradient is calculated. District production totals cannot be disaggregated into pixel-level recovery.

The Phase 3 pattern—state 2018–19 Autumn rice production 101,943 t, 31.54% below 2017–18; Winter and Summer rose—remains an observed seasonal contrast, not a flood effect. Dates of harvest, planting, area harvested, yield and production are distinct outcomes. A 2018 flood could affect different harvest seasons at different stages. The 2019 flood, season-specific weather, cropping shifts and price/policy changes can bias a simple post-2018 comparison. Additional 2020–23 DES annual reports were located but not transcribed here, because no credible exposure/control join has been established. Two post-event annual observations cannot measure recovery duration.

If an exposure join becomes defensible, pre-register the exposure metric and harvest-season lag, inspect pre-event parallel trends and 2016/2017 seasonal variability, compare genuinely exposed and less-exposed districts/blocks with matched agro-ecology and controls, include absolute tonnes/hectares and relative percentages, and use uncertainty intervals that account for spatial dependence and exposure error. A flood-associated deviation requires evidence against plausible controls; a post-flood change alone is not attributable. Later-event tests need predictors frozen before the held-out flood and spatial/temporal separation of training/selection and evaluation. Retrospective recovery priority and pre-event prediction must have different labels.

## Separate recovery-domain feasibility

| Domain | Candidate independently observed measure | Present status and important limits |
|---|---|---|
| Vegetation/moisture | Landsat/Sentinel-2 NDVI, EVI, NDMI; phenology-adjusted anomalies, persistent bare/low-cover area | An 8-month pilot image/QA availability screen was executed. No Sentinel-2 SR scenes were present in June–September 2017/2018. Sentinel-2 TOA exists, but QA60 rejects opaque cloud/cirrus only and its high non-cloud fraction is an optimistic upper bound, especially without shadows. Landsat 8 had zero July 2018 scenes and only 0.19% clear-any pilot coverage in August. No vegetation trajectory or crop-specific mask was estimated. |
| Economic activity | VIIRS night lights over stable, sufficiently lit settlements | Eight pilot monthly mean radiances and `cf_cvg` observation counts were computed. July/August 2018 mean cloud-free counts were 0.113/0.353 per pixel, and no stable-lit settlement mask was defined. Rural low-light floor, blooming, power outages and sensor noise prevent interpreting raw radiance change as income. |
| Infrastructure and households | Dated road reopenings, utility restoration, housing repair and household return records | Public operational reports may contain aggregates, but no georeferenced dated unit-level series was obtained. Administrative completion can differ from actual service or household functioning. |
| Official crop activity | DES district/block season area, production and yield | Phase 3 panel exists. Longer reports are available; seasonal definitions, corrected source transcription, local scale and other floods need harmonization. |

These domains have different units, resolution, observation processes and error. They must be analyzed separately before any composite index. The monthly availability values are **not** NDVI, EVI, NDMI or economic-recovery measurements. No weights were chosen and no ranking or ranking sensitivity grid was run in Phase 4.

## Reproducibility and uncertainty

The public-source screen uses fixed downloaded snapshots and Python standard library, with deterministic sorting and no random seed. The 913-event GFD match is based on the producer QC polygons' parsed bounding boxes and a deliberately broad Kerala search rectangle; no candidate can be rejected due to exact state-boundary detail if its bbox intersects. Its zero result is an event-catalog result, not evidence that Kerala did not flood. The Bhuvan `GetMap` bbox is the requested rendering footprint, not a satellite observation footprint. The candidate district GeoJSON is OSM-derived and temporally unverified. Browser auto-review blocked opening the researcher's general Google Drive view, but the researcher supplied the specific Earth Engine inventory CSV through a local download, so the complete scene audit could proceed. No personal account or auth material is stored.
