# Methods and limitations

## Gate and source selection

The independent 11 November 2026 Phase 6 audit was read from the researcher's Downloads folder and copied unmodified to `data/raw/`. It confirms that the 378-row Phase 6 product is a unique long-format transformation, and that the reported −50.15151 pp contrast is descriptive. Its no-go verdict on validated exposure and recovery is binding here.

For 2014–15 through 2019–20, the source is the read-only Phase 3 audited 252-row panel. Its compendium Table 17 labels seasonal *paddy area* without explicitly stating wet/dry scope; the 2019–20 annual report confirms those seasonal area cells as **wet paddy**, while its Table 4 labels output simply *Rice* without a wet/dry split. The Phase 3 production/area ratio is therefore retained only in `legacy_phase3_ratio_tonnes_per_ha`, not asserted as comparable yield. For 2020–21 through 2023–24, Phase 7 extracts wet-paddy area from DES Table 3 and **Rice (Total)** production from Table 4 (Table 8 in 2023–24), retaining its wet and dry rice components. This is the closest published production match to the earlier unsplit Rice series; strict component comparability across the report-format break remains unverified. The parser skips the preceding paddy-grain table in 2022–23 and 2023–24. The 2024–25 autumn/winter and summer final estimates list wet and dry separately; total rice is their sum. Both pages of the scanned summer PDF were visually transcribed. Only for 2020–21 onward is `yield_tonnes_per_ha` calculated from **wet rice / wet-paddy area**; it is blank before then or for zero wet area.

Area/production are harvest-season totals, not plant-level function observations. `Autumn`, `Winter` and `Summer` follow the reports' season labels and agricultural year, not a common calendar date for every field. The source does not provide planting/harvest dates per district. All reported zeros remain zero; blank yield may mean pre-2020 component mismatch or a zero-area ratio. The 2024–25 separate final estimates are a format/version shift, flagged in `source_decision`. The publication list offered 2025–26 autumn and winter estimates, but no complete three-season 2025–26 set at this audit; 2024–25 is the latest complete year used.

## Checks and descriptive analysis

`extend_rice_panel.py` requires 14 distinct district rows in each source table, normalises printed district spellings, checks 462 unique district–year–season keys and reports sums against state controls. It explicitly repairs two wrapped 2021–22 winter area cells (Kollam 1,177.08 ha; Pathanamthitta 217.24 ha), which were verified by the row totals and state reconciliation. It also checks wet+dry components against each printed total-rice district cell; three 2020–21 cells disagree by >0.01 t and are flagged, not silently repaired. Original PDFs and extraction text are retained. The 2024–25 component state rows reconcile before wet+dry rice are combined; the summer wet-area difference of 0.005 ha is consistent with state rounding.

`descriptive_diagnostics.py` recomputes the 2018–19 autumn production percentage departure from each district's own 2014–15 to 2017–18 same-season mean, then contrasts the official 2018 flood/**landslide** administrative groups. It also computes a 2017–18 placebo against the first three pre-event years, descriptive four-point pre-event slopes, and three alternative baselines (median, first-three-year mean, linear extrapolation) alongside the four-year mean. These diagnostics use **area and production separately**; historical derived yield is excluded because its component scope cannot be validated. No weight or threshold is optimised. Percent changes are omitted for nonpositive baselines; absolute values remain available. The four-point extrapolation is a sensitivity illustration, not a validated forecast.

## Scientific limits

- Administrative notification is neither measured paddy inundation nor randomly assigned treatment. The source panel lacks observed field exposure. Its groups are not exchangeable; a label-permutation statistic from Phase 6 is not a causal p-value.
- The 2017–18 placebo contrast of +19.25802 pp warns against assuming parallel group trajectories. Several small baseline districts have volatile percentages. Report absolute tonnes and district values as well as unweighted percentages.
- 2019, 2020 and 2021 have official later flood/event evidence; 2022–24 were not systematically classified as flood-free. The 2024 Meppadi landslide is a distinct hazard. COVID and policy, weather, crop calendars, dry/wet composition, yield survey error and reporting revisions may affect later totals.
- Source state sums are control checks, not proof of measurement accuracy. A 2020–21 printed **total-rice** state row differs from the sum of district cells by up to 0.074 t. The 2024–25 scanned summer wet/dry tables are verified by visual transcription and separate state reconciliation.
- Phase 4 optical vegetation and VIIRS radiance are proxies with sparse relevant observations, not validated functional recovery. A statewide electricity-restoration milestone has no local service-duration panel. Housing/road damage and expenditure are not dated return-to-function outcomes.
- No Phase 7 exposure-overlap pilot, flood-mask accuracy statistic, attributable recovery duration, multidimensional index or rank-benefit test was run. Those require independent geospatial labels with observed support, contemporaneous paddy geography and historical unit alignment.
