# Exposure-to-recovery linkage: executed screen and blocked spatial join

## Intended spatial link and present limit

The Phase 4 pilot is a 921 × 1,111 grid at 30 m in EPSG:32643. Tiwari et al.'s 21 August 2018 GeoTIFF is a real georeferenced **water-positive** product that overlaps it. We resampled its value-1 cells to the pilot grid by nearest neighbour, preserving zero/NoData as unknown. The pilot grid contains 1,023,231 cells; 394,423 are Tiwari-positive at 21 August. Among Phase 4's 238,477 strict-QA cells, 218,573 are Tiwari-positive. Phase 4's 3 dB candidate is positive on 126,941 of these, **58.0772% conditional same-source concordance**. This is **not recall or accuracy**: Tiwari is also derived from Sentinel-1, has no valid negative class/scene footprint, and detects water including potentially permanent/seasonal water. The 9 and 21 August scenes are different event times; no pooling was done. Other date counts are in `results/tiwari_phase4_positive_overlap.csv`, where January already has 197,432 positives and 28 July has 343,430 on the pilot grid. These are positive map pixels on a common output grid, not confirmed new flooded hectares.

We did **not** convert positive pixels to affected rice hectares or district exposure fractions. A flooded-rice fraction needs an event-specific validated inundation mask, its surveyed positive and negative/unknown footprint, a dated 2018 cultivated-rice-by-season map, and 2018-valid district/block boundaries. No such mutually valid join exists locally. Raster bbox area cannot stand in for sensor coverage; NoData zeros cannot stand in for non-flooded controls. Using Tiwari as an external truth layer against our Sentinel-1 mask would also violate measurement independence.

## Executed district-level alternative

The official 3 October 2018 notification provides a **flood-or-landslide administrative severity proxy**: seven districts with all villages listed, six with some villages listed, and one (Kasaragod) not listed. `scripts/notification_rice_screen.py` links this status by exact district name to the independently audited Phase 3 district × harvest-season panel (252 observations, 2014-15 through 2019-20). It saves 378 district × season × metric × comparison rows and 27 group summaries.

Predeclared primary descriptive outcome: 2018-19 **Autumn** (July–October harvest) rice production deviation from each district's same-season 2014-15–2017-18 mean. Six districts in each administrative group have nonzero autumn production baselines. Wayanad, though in the all-villages group, has zero autumn baseline and is excluded from percent summaries; Kasaragod is omitted from group comparison because `not listed` is not an observed low-exposure measurement. The six all-villages districts average **−65.3911%**; the six some-villages districts average **−15.2396%**; the contrast is **−50.1515 percentage points**. Grouped absolute deviations from baseline are **−42,604.75 t** and **−13,997.50 t** respectively. Aggregate output relative to group baseline is **−87.1642%** versus **−13.1182%**, dominated by large producers (notably Alappuzha/Kottayam and Palakkad). These are changes in total district rice output, not rice-area inundation.

For transparency, an exact two-sided permutation of the 12 observed district labels gives descriptive `p=0.017316`; its labels are **not exchangeable by design**, so this is not a causal/randomization test. Leave-one-district-out contrasts range from **−57.1707 to −44.1645 pp**. The 2017-18 pre-event placebo contrast, against 2014-15–2016-17 means, was **+19.2580 pp** for production; this is evidence that the groups already followed different paths. The 2019-20 contrast remains **−26.3201 pp**, but another Kerala flood occurred in 2019 and this does not identify recovery duration. In 2018-19 autumn, area change differs by **+4.3001 pp** while yield change differs by **−51.7996 pp**, showing why area, yield and production must remain separate. For tiny baseline producers, percent changes are unstable; all district values and baselines are retained in the CSV. No model assumes a two-year average is recovery duration.

The administrative notification is broad and includes landslides, rainfall-related damage and policy designation. It is neither a randomized exposure nor measured field inundation. Districts differ in paddy ecology, crop calendar, rainfall, dams, aid, pretrend and 2019 shock. A six-versus-six comparison cannot establish parallel trends or flood attribution. The output association is an **exploratory district screening result**, not the objective's observation-level spatial exposure–recovery link. The Phase 3 harvest year is not a monthly field trajectory; later-year output need not indicate recovery of the same fields.

## Required validation before advancing

With the NRSC/KSDMA RADARSAT-2 event raster and surveyed footprint (or another independently observed labeled source), align exact UTC and geographic overlap with Sentinel-1 and Tiwari; separate permanent water and unknown; evaluate positive and negative labels with spatially blocked uncertainty. Then intersect independently verified inundation with a contemporaneous cultivated-rice map and historically valid unit boundaries. Only after exposing the fraction of rice under a valid common denominator can matched district/block seasonal outcomes be compared, checking pretrends, 2019 flooding, rainfall and programmes. The current data cannot satisfy that design.
