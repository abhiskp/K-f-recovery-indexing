# Methods and limitations

## Source audit and geospatial rules

The Phase 5 decision gate is binding: no Phase 4 Sentinel-1 threshold raster is a validated flood extent. Phase 6 audited each alternate source's archive members, coordinate system, class codes, nodata, dates, pilot overlap, acquisition and processing provenance, licence, and dependence on Phase 4 imagery. External MD5s were checked for Zenodo and NIH/PMC files; all local files receive SHA-256 in `results/checksums.csv`.

The Sentinel Asia KMZ uses KML WGS84 bounding rectangles and RGBA cartographic overlays. The alpha mask is rendering support, **not** a satellite observed-footprint class. Tiwari S1 has georeferenced water-positive value-1 cells but explicitly marks zero as NoData. We used nearest-neighbour reprojection of 1s to the exact Phase 4 30 m grid and counted overlaps only. Since zero has no known survey meaning, we calculated neither true negatives nor flood-area fractions. The conditional 58.0772% is same-source overlap, not recall. Coordinate resampling and 30 m cell counts do not remove geolocation uncertainty; no independent spatial uncertainty interval is estimable without labels and footprints.

Tiwari et al. use Sentinel-1 VV, the same modality/likely event acquisitions as Phase 4, and their paper reports that cloud-obscured optical reference samples were selected using Sentinel-1. Their published reported accuracy cannot be imported as independent evidence for our candidate. The archive does not contain their exact scene IDs, non-water sample locations or class frame. Nor does it distinguish newly flooded from pre-existing water in the supplied files. The published filenames are date labels, not exact UTC timestamps. A time-matched ALOS-2 layer would be preferable, but the public KMZ covers a different geography and lacks negatives/QA. The IFI inventory's broad event coding and historical district tables cannot substitute for event footprints.

## District notification/rice analysis

The only executed cross-domain join uses **district name** between the official flood/landslide village order and Phase 3's 252-row DES rice panel. It retains all three harvest seasons and area, production, yield separately. For each district-season-metric, 2018-19 and 2019-20 observations are compared to the arithmetic mean of the four prior same seasons (2014-15 through 2017-18). A 2017-18 placebo compares to the prior three seasons. Absolute deviation is observed minus baseline; percent deviation is `100 × (observed / baseline − 1)` only when baseline is nonzero. Blank means undefined/unavailable, not zero. Published zero rice values remain zero. The main contrast is the difference between the two groups' unweighted district mean percent deviations. A group-total production/area change uses sums of levels, not a mean of yields. The exact label permutation enumerates all combinations among districts with defined percentages; it is a descriptive reference distribution because category assignment is neither random nor plausibly exchangeable. Leave-one-out reports sensitivity to deleting one included district, not a confidence interval.

The 3 October notification amends an earlier 29 September count; we use the amended category without treating `not listed` as a known dry comparator. The order combines flood and landslide designation and gives no depth, inundation fraction or paddy overlap. Its seven all-village districts include Wayanad, which has no autumn rice baseline; only six all-village districts enter the autumn percent contrast. Small district production baselines can magnify percentages, so absolute tonnage and every district level are saved. The pre-event placebo difference is nonzero, warning that these groups differ even before the event. 2019–20 rice output is subject to the 2019 flood and potentially different harvest timing/aid and does not measure recovery time of 2018 fields. Four pre-event annual harvest observations do not adequately model weather trends or farmer adaptation. We make no causal effect estimate.

## Other recovery dimensions

Agricultural DES statistics are real district-season observations; 2020-21 and later official reports exist but were not harmonized in Phase 6 because a valid exposed/control definition is missing. Landsat 8 and Sentinel-2 can form seasonal vegetation/moisture series in principle, but Phase 4's actual pilot query found extremely low clear August 2018 support and no relevant Sentinel-2 SR scenes. Atmospheric/cloud-shadow QA and matched phenology would be required before NDVI/EVI/NDMI recovery. VIIRS is monthly, coarser and sparse under monsoon cloud; raw radiance is not income and needs stable lit settlements and valid `cf_cvg` counts. The 2019 biodiversity field assessment identifies post-flood riparian impact but does not supply repeated pre/post field measurements in its public report. Kerala's electricity restoration milestone is a state aggregate, not geographically dated functionality; road/housing damage totals or spending are also not restored-service trajectories. No cross-domain index was created.

## Future inference boundary

An exposure–recovery analysis requires independently verified, dated 2018 inundation with a valid observed footprint, dated 2018 cultivated-paddy polygons, contemporary boundaries, and later measured outcomes. It must hold season and geography constant, account for 2017/2019 flooding and rainfall, inspect pretrends and support, and separate retrospective priority from prospective prediction. Ranking weights, rank shifts and held-out benefit cannot be defended until those measurements and independent outcomes exist. No threshold was tuned on Tiwari positives and no confusion matrix was calculated from NoData zeros.
